## Current UI and UX Condition

The platform now presents a stage-aware booking experience across customer web, customer mobile, and expert mobile, built around a single visibility contract (`bookingJourney.js`) that decides what the user sees at each lifecycle step rather than dumping every control on screen at once. On the customer side, the web experience opens with a modern marketing landing page—“Relief, Delivered”—with immersive hero, service discovery, trust signals, and a sticky mobile CTA, then flows into authenticated browsing, cart, and checkout. Booking supports a hybrid payment model: **pay now** keeps the customer on a payment-first screen with no map, timeline, or dispatch animation until payment clears, while **pay later** dispatches immediately and surfaces softer payment reminders mid-journey if the session is still unpaid. The tracking page is driven entirely by journey visibility flags: the hero owns the primary narrative (no duplicate status badges), the timeline correctly highlights “Finding expert” during search rather than prematurely showing “Expert assigned,” the map appears only when contextually appropriate (address pin while searching, live tracking when the expert is en route, hidden during payment, in-session, completion, or cancellation), expert cards appear only after assignment, cancel is limited to early stages, and order list cards show concise secondary lines (“Payment due,” “~12 min away,” “Session in progress”) with a live chip only for true live-tracking statuses. The customer mobile app mirrors this contract in `BookingScreen` with status-driven headers and gated sections—payment-only UI for `awaiting_payment`, search-focused UI without a map for `created`/`searching`, full map and ETA for `assigned`, session timer and add-ons without map or cancel for `in_progress`, and receipt-style completion or cancellation summaries at the end—while `CartScreen` now requires a real GPS fix before checkout instead of silently falling back to a default city. On the expert side, incoming dispatch offers show distance, ETA, service duration, full address, and an estimated earning figure (not the customer’s total bill), the active job screen walks through a clear stepper (navigate → arrived confirmation → start OTP → session timer → end OTP → completion card with earnings), customer pricing is hidden during the trip, OTP dev hints appear only in development builds, and completed or cancelled orders from history open a read-only detail view rather than the active job screen. Real-time updates flow over Socket.IO for status changes, arrival, add-ons, and live location; maps use Leaflet with OpenStreetMap tiles on mobile and equivalent map components on web. Copy, labels, and CTAs have been aligned across surfaces (“Continue to payment,” “Confirm & find expert,” “Expert assigned,” pay-before-session-end urgency for unpaid pay-later sessions). What remains intentionally lightweight or stubbed includes real Razorpay/Stripe checkout UI, in-app masked calling, a full rating screen, push notifications when backgrounded, geofenced arrival, and some legacy expert screens (`JobScreen`, `JobsScreen`) that are superseded by `ActiveOrderScreen` but not yet removed.

## Platform Intention and What Exists Today

The platform is intended to be a hyperlocal, on-demand wellness marketplace—an Urban Company–style experience focused on quick physical relief at home—with a **15-minute arrival SLA**, transparent pricing, verified experts, and a product promise summarized as making everyday physical relief accessible, affordable, and available within minutes. It is built as a monorepo with a Node.js/Express/MongoDB backend (JWT auth, phone OTP, H3-based expert dispatch, haversine ETA ranking, session OTP verification, earnings commission logic, and Socket.IO realtime rooms), a **customer web** app (Vite/React) for discovery, booking, and live order tracking, **customer** and **expert** Expo/React Native mobile apps for field use, and an **admin web** (Next.js) for catalog and expert management. Customers can log in via OTP, browse categorized wellness services (head, neck, shoulder, back, leg relief, and related offerings), combine multiple services into one booking, choose pay-now or pay-later at checkout, track their expert in real time, add services mid-session, pay, and rate. Experts can go online/offline, receive timed dispatch offers matched by skill and proximity, navigate to the customer, confirm arrival, verify start/end OTPs shared by the customer, complete sessions, and view earnings on their dashboard and order history. The backend dispatcher grows H3 rings until it finds qualified online experts within the SLA, offers jobs sequentially with timeout/reassign behavior, and guards pay-now bookings from dispatch until payment is confirmed. Together, these pieces form an end-to-end marketplace loop—from marketing and conversion through dispatch, live fulfillment, payment, and completion—designed to feel as immediate and legible as consumer delivery apps (Zepto/Rapido-style clarity on the customer side, ride-hailing-style job flow on the expert side) while staying purpose-built for short, at-home wellness sessions rather than medical care.
